# Current lead-to-deal links

Place the reader and its native fixture in `src/features/stats/`. The reader takes an authenticated request actor, the existing report scope, an abort signal, and the optional request locale fallback. It rehydrates current authority within one read-only repeatable-read transaction and retains the request’s permission ceiling. All-pipelines scope is required because a lead has no pipeline before conversion.

The population comprises current visible, nondeleted leads with a stored creation date inside the viewer’s selected civil period. Current lead owner and stored lead currency define those report scopes. Archived leads remain eligible because conversion archives the source lead. Destination linkage requires the recorded direct pointer and an independently visible, unarchived and nondeleted deal in a current visible pipeline. Current destination ownership and currency do not change the source lead cohort. No destination financial or contact fields enter the report.

Every eligible lead enters one bucket. A current linked lead has both authorized endpoints. An active lead without a recorded pointer is pending linkage. An archived lead without a pointer has unknown handoff outcome. A recorded pointer without an eligible visible destination is unavailable without stating why. The aggregate counts distinct lead rows and distinct visible destination deals separately. It never follows merge, reverse-conversion or contact links. No entity keys or authored fields are returned.

`convertLead` creates the deal, writes the direct pointer, archives the lead and records a `convertedDealId` change log in one transaction. Bulk conversion uses that same path. A generic change log can corroborate an application write. Its transaction-time clock, absence of a dedicated sequence or coverage boundary, and polymorphic field identity do not establish a qualified handoff event stream. Archive and update clocks can change. Deal-to-lead conversion records separate reverse references. The private capture proposal currently observes deals, stages and pipelines. It does not record the lead write and its lifecycle as a qualified chain.

Qualification, acceptance, rejection, repeated or returned handoffs and first response require explicit durable event kinds, actor and owner policy, reason, event-time semantics, deduplication and observation coverage. Carried activities and messages do not establish the first qualifying response. This slice exposes neither those histories nor a historical channel, handoff date, response time or conversion rate. Its dates describe lead creation, and the linkage is evaluated at the current authorized read.
